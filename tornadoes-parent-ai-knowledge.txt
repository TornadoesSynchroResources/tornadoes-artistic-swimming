National Team Pathway, Values & Real-World Resilience

1
Your Journey is Individual — Personal Responsibility
Representing the club and striving for a spot on the Jamaica National Artistic Swimming Team is one of the highest and most demanding honors an athlete can pursue. Although Artistic Swimming is a team sport, the journey toward national team selection is walked by each athlete individually.
No One Can Train for You
Leg height, flexibility, technical precision, lung capacity, and mental toughness depend 100% on your individual effort in every practice.
Autonomy & Maturity
Preparing for the national team means taking ownership of nutrition, rest, punctuality, body care, and physical preparation both in and out of the pool.
Your Attitude Defines Your Progress
During trial evaluations, judges look beyond technical execution. Discipline, character, and the ability to perform under pressure are part of becoming a high-performance athlete.
2
Discipline & Values of a Tornadoes Athlete
Value
What It Means Day-to-Day
Unwavering Discipline
Give 100% on every repetition, even when tired or when no one is watching. Follow schedules and training plans without excuses.
Respect & Esprit de Corps
Respect coaches, teammates, and officials. Support clubmates and understand that healthy internal competition elevates everyone.
Consistency
High performance is not achieved in one perfect practice, but through weeks and months of steady, uninterrupted effort.
Humility & Receptivity
Receive technical corrections with an open mind. Every critique is meant to refine execution.
3
The Reality of High-Performance Training
Aiming for the national team requires elevating how you approach daily training. Artistic Swimming demands development both in and out of the pool.
Landwork & Flexibility
Leg and back flexibility, core strength, and acrobatic/gymnastic conditioning are as essential as pool-focused repetition.
Technical Mastery
Precision in height, angles, rotation speed, and buoyancy control requires deliberate repetition and refinement toward international-level execution.
Mental Resilience
Pushing through fatigue while maintaining composure during selection trials is a key part of high-performance preparation.
4
Failure as a Catalyst — Subjectivity & Real Champions
“Failure is not the opposite of success; it is a vital part of it. If a trial or score doesn't go as expected, it is simply a starting point to refine your craft and return stronger.”
Understanding Subjectivity
Artistic Swimming is different from objective sports such as track & field or speed swimming. Angles, height, and artistic expression can look different from the deck than from the judges' panel. A mature athlete accepts an official evaluation without bitterness.
Failing Forward is Necessary
Missing a figure, receiving a lower score than expected, or receiving a Base Mark is not a signal that you lack talent. It is feedback that identifies the precision needed to grow.
Real-World Resilience in Artistic Swimming
Examples included in the Tornadoes National Team Pathway document.
Bill May (USA)
Excluded from Olympic competition for decades because of gender restrictions. After rules changed, he was cut from the Paris 2024 roster; he continued building an iconic career and helped open doors for male artistic swimmers.
Anita Alvarez (USA)
After severe fainting episodes in competition, she refocused on training, mental health, and physical limits and returned to win Olympic Silver at Paris 2024.
Jacqueline Simoneau (Canada)
Stepped away from the national team because of a toxic training environment and psychological pressure, studied medicine, focused on mental health, and later returned to lead Canada's Olympic duet.
Megumi Field (USA)
Faced technical pressure while adapting to the Degree of Difficulty judging system and transformed her approach to master greater technical precision.
Daniella Ramirez (USA)
Overcame severe shoulder injuries and adaptation struggles and developed into a major acrobatic contributor on the world stage.
Fedra Cazaubon (Mexico)
After being moved to the alternate bench during national team restructuring, she embraced her supporting role, built a strong work ethic, and fought her way back into starting lineups.
Glenda Inzunza (Mexico)
After national sports funding was cut, she became self-managed, selling swimsuits and pursuing legal avenues to secure her opportunity to compete internationally.
Itzamary González (Mexico)
Lost world podium opportunities after Base Mark penalties under the new scoring system and used the experience to master mathematical precision and composure under pressure.
Chloe McClymont (Australia)
Faced a lack of national funding and high-performance infrastructure, funded her process and moved far from home to continue pursuing international artistic swimming.
Regina Alférez (Mexico)
Endured severe injuries that challenged her ability to sustain high-volume national training. Through rehabilitation she overcame fears of premature retirement and returned as a reliable starter.

National Qualification Structure — ASAJ Framework 2026–2027
Three performance tiers described in the pathway document.
Development Squad
Entry-level international pathway designed to gain regional competition experience.
National Team
Selection to represent Jamaica at official competitions such as CARIFTA, CCCAN, and PanAm Aquatics.
Elite National Team
Highest performance tier, focused on international podiums, World Cups, and World Championships.
ASAJ Technical & Degree of Difficulty (DD) Criteria
To qualify, athletes must achieve the required average score in Figures/Technical Elements and Routine, as well as execute the minimum required Degree of Difficulty (DD).
ASAJ Tiers and Criteria
12 & Under — Development Squad: Required Avg Score 5.0; Minimum Executed DD 10.0
12 & Under — National Team: Required Avg Score 5.4; Minimum Executed DD 14.8
12 & Under — Elite National Team: Required Avg Score 5.0; Minimum Executed DD 20.0
Youth (13–15) — Development Squad: Required Avg Score 5.4; Minimum Executed DD 14.0
Youth (13–15) — National Team: Required Avg Score 5.8; Minimum Executed DD 18.5
Youth (13–15) — Elite National Team: Required Avg Score 5.4; Minimum Executed DD 25.0

Junior — National Team: Required Avg Score 6.2; Minimum Executed DD 20.2

Senior — National Team: Required Avg Score 6.6; Minimum Executed DD 20.2

BASE MARK GUIDE
Base Mark is not a judgment of talent; it means declared difficulty was not achieved as declared.
Coach Card is the origin for the declared difficulty and technical verification.
Possible triggers include omitted movement, different technique, incomplete rotation, required height/position/criterion not achieved, or wrong order.
Do What You Declare.
If a free hybrid is not achieved as declared, Base Mark is 0.50.
Athletes should use video to identify height, rotation, position, timing, connection, or order issues.
Coaches should decide whether difficulty matches current control and clean or adjust the declaration.
Tornadoes mindset: What exactly happened? Why did it happen? What do we need to change?

CATEGORIES
Supernovice — Foundation Year: initial and most basic competitive level; purpose is first competitions without high technical pressure; age subdivisions may include 8&U, 12&U, 13&Over according to competition structure; short/simple routines and basic figures such as Tub 360 and Somersault Back Tuck; advanced skills may require moving to Novice/Intermediate under applicable ASAJ rules; foundation focuses on alignment, positions, pointed feet, sculling, coordination, water confidence, flexibility, strength and fundamental movement patterns. Foundation First. Progression Follows.
Novice — Beginner: generally first or second year; basic required figures/body positions/controlled transitions; mainly team routines at local/recreational events, with Solo/Duet when appropriate; goal is reliable technical habits and preparation for next level.
Intermediate — Development: more than one year; bridge between basic recreation and high performance; more complex figures, transitions and positions; Teams, Duets and Solos with synchronization, aerobic strength, fluid transitions; goal is consistency and technical readiness.
Key difference: Novice less than 1 year/early development, fundamental positions, introduction to competition; Intermediate more than 1 year, more complex transitions/positions, preparation for advanced competition.
Age groups: 12&U, Youth, Junior, Senior. Age group and technical level are related but are not the same classification.
Competition rules used for Tornadoes depend on category and competition summons.
Supernovice: per competition summons; free Hybrid allowed; no acrobatics; foundation focus.
Jamaica Novice & Intermediate follow USA Artistic Swimming rules applicable to those levels; check specific competition summons.
Novice: maximum 1 free Hybrid per routine; no acrobatics or platforms; must meet applicable 5 Technical Required Elements; extra Hybrid may receive excess-element penalty.
Intermediate: Solo max 1 free Hybrid; Duet/Mixed Duet max 2 free Hybrids OR 1 free Hybrid + 1 Pair Acrobatics; Team max 2 free Hybrids OR 1 free Hybrid + 1 Team Acrobatics.
Youth: Solo Free 5 hybrids/0 acro; Duet Free 6/0; Mixed Duet Free 5/0 + 3 surface connections; Team Free 8 total elements, acro subject to safety/DD; Free Combination 9 total elements. Youth/12U Solo/Duet/Mixed Duet strictly no acrobatics; group acrobatics only Team/Free Combination. Youth free hybrid apnea limit 21 sec.
12&U: Solo Free 4/0; Duet Free 5/0; Mixed Duet Free 5/0 + 3 surface connections; Team Free 7 total elements; Free Combination 8 total elements.
User-supplied Hub claim: a hybrid requires at least 5 underwater movements with head submerged.
CATEGORIES & PATHWAY GUIDE
Progression: FOUNDATION → DEVELOPMENT → NATIONAL → ELITE.
How pathway works: Supernovice, Novice and Intermediate training establish technical skills, artistry, physical literacy, confidence and competition experience before 12&Under, Youth, Junior and Senior pathways. International pathway: Development Squad → National Team → Elite National Team, subject to official ASAJ eligibility and qualification standards.
Supernovices: establish alignment, body positions, pointed feet, sculling, water confidence, flexibility, strength and fundamental movement patterns.
Novices/Intermediate: build stronger technical control, figures, transitions, propulsion, flexibility and competition readiness.
Age Group 12&Under/Youth/Junior/Senior: progressively higher technical, routine and competitive standards.
12&Under: competition-ready figure quality, sculling strength, height, flexibility and clean lines.
Youth: increase technical difficulty, height, flexibility, hybrid understanding and competitive presentation.
Junior: advanced elements, precision, speed, routine execution and National Team preparation.
Senior: highest domestic age-group demands with emphasis on international-level execution and difficulty.
