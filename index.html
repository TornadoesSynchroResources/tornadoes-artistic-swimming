Your Journey is Individual (Personal Responsibility)

Although Artistic Swimming is a team sport where we shine together, national selection is walked by each athlete individually.

No One Can Train For You

Leg height, flexibility, technical precision, lung capacity, and mental toughness depend 100% on your individual effort in every single practice.

Autonomy & Maturity

Preparing for the national team means taking full ownership of your nutrition, rest, punctuality, body care, and physical preparation both in and out of the pool.

Attitude Defines Progress

During trial evaluations, judges look beyond technical execution—they assess discipline, character, and your ability to perform under high pressure.

Discipline & Core Values of a Tornadoes Athlete

Talent alone is not enough to wear Tornadoes colors and represent Jamaica. It requires a solid foundation of core values:

Unwavering Discipline

Giving 100% on every repetition, even when tired or when no one is watching. Adhering to schedules and training plans without excuses.

Respect & Esprit de Corps

Respecting coaches, teammates, and officials. Supporting clubmates, remembering that healthy internal competition elevates everyone.

Consistency

High performance is not achieved in a single perfect practice, but over weeks and months of steady, uninterrupted effort.

Humility & Receptivity

Receiving technical corrections with an open mind, understanding that every critique is meant to refine your execution.

Reality of High-Performance Training

  • Landwork & Flexibility: Leg and back flexibility, core strength, and acrobatic conditioning are just as essential as pool time.
  • Technical Mastery: Precision in height, angles, rotation speed, and buoyancy control requires deliberate, focused repetition.
  • Mental Resilience: Pushing through fatigue and maintaining composure during selection trials is key for international-level athletes.

Subjectivity & Failing Forward

Artistic swimming is a sport of subjective appreciation—there is no stopwatch or finish line. Angles and height look different from the judges' panel than from the deck.

Accepting subjective evaluation without bitterness is the mark of a mature athlete. Receiving a lower score or a Base Mark is essential feedback, highlighting the exact millimeter of precision needed to grow.

Pioneer Inspiration: Bill May (USA)

Excluded from Olympic competition for decades due to gender rules. Instead of quitting, he performed in Cirque du Soleil, kept pushing boundaries, and became the pioneer who opened doors for male artistic swimmers globally.

Parents are the emotional anchor of our athletes. Your support, calm guidance, and encouragement of autonomy are essential for your daughter's long-term success on the National Team Pathway.

— Tornadoes Parent Support Charter

1. Be an Emotional Safe Harbor

Trial preparation is mentally taxing. Praise effort, resilience, and discipline rather than placing pressure on scores or final squad placements.

2. Encourage Athlete Autonomy

Allow your athlete to manage her swim bag, gear, water bottle, and punctuality. Autonomy in dryland builds character and judges recognize independent maturity.

3. Model Respect for Judging

Help your athlete view judge feedback and Base Marks as constructive guidance. When parents handle scores with poise, athletes bounce back faster and stronger.

Official 2026 Evaluation Standards

Technical Evaluation Forms & Digital Catalogues

Review the 10 Figure elements (F1-F10), 10 Routine elements (R1-R10), and 5 Land elements (L1-L5) evaluated by Staff.

General Evaluation Conditions (Rules 1 to 12)

1. Attire Code: Unmarked black suit and white cap for all in-water evaluations.
2. Complete Assessment: All 25 elements (F1-F10, R1-R10, L1-L5) must be attempted and scored.
3. Overall Score Calculation: Average of the 25 cells multiplied by 10 (e.g. Average 5.8 = Overall score 58).
4. Passing Benchmark: Average ≥ 5.0 required for Bronze Rating (Juniors require International Target ≥ 7.0).
5. Failure Threshold: If more than 2 elements have a score less than 4.5 (or ≤ 5.5 in Juniors), it is an automatic fail.
6. Stationarity Rule: Movements performed relatively stationary and in uniform motion unless noted otherwise.
7. Single Attempt Standard: Assessments mimic real competition; swimmers are allowed only ONE attempt per element.
8. Dry Land Wear: Stretch shorts/leggings required for land skills (L1-L5). No jeans permitted.
9. Coaching Feedback: Coaches highlight areas needing proficiency in official comments box.
10. No Replays Policy: No replays offered unless explicitly requested by head coach.
11. World Aquatics Standards: All figures judged strictly following current World Aquatics judging guide.
12. Communication Protocol: Any evaluation concerns must be communicated directly to coach in charge.

World Aquatics (WA) Official Competition Code & Rules

International scoring system, Coach Card declarations, hybrid difficulty families, and penalties.

Elements Panel (5 Judges)

Evaluates declared Technical Required Elements (TRE), Free Hybrids, and Acrobatics. Any under-rotation over 90° or missed declared element triggers an automatic Base Mark (BM) penalty.

Artistic Impression (5 Judges)

Evaluates Choreography, Music Interpretation, Manner of Presentation, and Transitions. Focuses on fluidity, expressiveness, and artistic execution between technical hybrids.

Technical Controllers (DTC & STC)

3 Difficulty Technical Controllers verify if declared movements match the Coach Card. 3 Synchronization Technical Controllers detect and log timing errors among athletes.
