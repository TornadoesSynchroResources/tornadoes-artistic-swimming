Your Journey is Individual — Personal Responsibility

Representing the club and striving for a spot on the Jamaica National Artistic Swimming Team is one of the highest and most demanding honors an athlete can pursue. Although Artistic Swimming is a team sport, the journey toward national team selection is walked by each athlete individually.

No One Can Train for You

Leg height, flexibility, technical precision, lung capacity, and mental toughness depend 100% on your individual effort in every practice.

Autonomy & Maturity

Preparing for the national team means taking ownership of nutrition, rest, punctuality, body care, and physical preparation both in and out of the pool.

Your Attitude Defines Your Progress

During trial evaluations, judges look beyond technical execution. Discipline, character, and the ability to perform under pressure are part of becoming a high-performance athlete.

The Reality of High-Performance Training

Aiming for the national team requires elevating how you approach daily training. Artistic Swimming demands development both in and out of the pool.

Landwork & Flexibility

Leg and back flexibility, core strength, and acrobatic/gymnastic conditioning are as essential as pool-focused repetition.

Technical Mastery

Precision in height, angles, rotation speed, and buoyancy control requires deliberate repetition and refinement toward international-level execution.

Mental Resilience

Pushing through fatigue while maintaining composure during selection trials is a key part of high-performance preparation.

4

Failure as a Catalyst — Subjectivity & Real Champions

“Failure is not the opposite of success; it is a vital part of it. If a trial or score doesn't go as expected, it is simply a starting point to refine your craft and return stronger.”

Understanding Subjectivity

Artistic Swimming is different from objective sports such as track & field or speed swimming. Angles, height, and artistic expression can look different from the deck than from the judges' panel. A mature athlete accepts an official evaluation without bitterness.

Failing Forward is Necessary

Missing a figure, receiving a lower score than expected, or receiving a Base Mark is not a signal that you lack talent. It is feedback that identifies the precision needed to grow.

Real-World Resilience in Artistic Swimming

Examples included in the Tornadoes National Team Pathway document.

Bill May (USA)

Excluded from Olympic competition for decades because of gender restrictions. After rules changed, he was cut from the Paris 2024 roster; he continued building an iconic career and helped open doors for male artistic swimmers.

Anita Alvarez (USA)

After severe fainting episodes in competition, she refocused on training, mental health, and physical limits and returned to win Olympic Silver at Paris 2024.

Jacqueline Simoneau (Canada)

Stepped away from the national team because of a toxic training environment and psychological pressure, studied medicine, focused on mental health, and later returned to lead Canada's Olympic duet.

Megumi Field (USA)

Faced technical pressure while adapting to the Degree of Difficulty judging system and transformed her approach to master greater technical precision.

Daniella Ramirez (USA)

Overcame severe shoulder injuries and adaptation struggles and developed into a major acrobatic contributor on the world stage.

Fedra Cazaubon (Mexico)

After being moved to the alternate bench during national team restructuring, she embraced her supporting role, built a strong work ethic, and fought her way back into starting lineups.

Glenda Inzunza (Mexico)

After national sports funding was cut, she became self-managed, selling swimsuits and pursuing legal avenues to secure her opportunity to compete internationally.

Itzamary González (Mexico)

Lost world podium opportunities after Base Mark penalties under the new scoring system and used the experience to master mathematical precision and composure under pressure.

Chloe McClymont (Australia)

Faced a lack of national funding and high-performance infrastructure, funded her process and moved far from home to continue pursuing international artistic swimming.

Regina Alférez (Mexico)

Endured severe injuries that challenged her ability to sustain high-volume national training. Through rehabilitation she overcame fears of premature retirement and returned as a reliable starter.

Technical Execution, Coach Cards & Base Marks

Coach Card & Declared DD

Before competition, coaches submit a Coach Card detailing every acrobatic movement and hybrid sequence in the exact order of execution. Declared DD is the theoretical mathematical value based on declared rotation, flexibility, and connection values.

Execution & Precision

High declared difficulty only counts when executed with precision. Height, extension, clarity, and synchronization determine whether declared values are validated by Technical Controllers.

Base Mark (BM)

A Base Mark occurs when an executed hybrid does not match the exact movement pattern, rotation, or declared criteria on the Coach Card. The declared high-difficulty score is revoked and the hybrid is reset to the minimum default baseline of 0.50 DD.

Reframing Base Marks — A Growth Mindset

A Base Mark is not a negative mark on an athlete's talent. It provides precise feedback, pinpointing where technique deteriorated under pressure. Pushing high-difficulty routines naturally increases Base Mark risk during early trial phases; overcoming these errors builds resilient, technically elite competitors.

Artistic Impression — World Aquatics Code

50/50 balance: Artistic Impression accounts for 50% of the total routine score in Free routines, with Technical Execution & Degree of Difficulty accounting for the remaining 50%.

Choreography & Musicality: creative movement patterns, full pool coverage, diversity of design, and precise synchronization to musical accents and tempo changes.
Performance & Expression: facial projection, energy, charisma, emotional delivery, and overall impact from deck movement to pool exit.
Transitions: fluidity, connection, and technical complexity of pathways linking hybrids, elements, and acrobatics.

World Aquatics (WA) Rules & Judging Guide

A practical guide for Tornadoes athletes and families: Elements, Synchronization, Artistic Impression, Transitions, Coach Cards, Base Marks and Hybrid Difficulty.

Athlete & Parent Guide

How a Routine Is Evaluated

This is a simplified explanation of the World Aquatics Artistic Swimming Manual for Judges, Technical Controllers, Referees & Coaches.

Elements

Elements are the technical skills performed in the routine: Technical Required Elements, Hybrids and Acrobatics where applicable. The Elements Judges focus on execution — how well the athlete masters and performs the chosen skill.

Synchronization

In Duet and Team routines, synchronization means that athletes perform movements at the same time and with the same intended design. Differences in timing, body position, height, spacing or pattern shape can create synchronization errors.

Synchronization — What Athletes Should Understand

Synchronization is more than simply "moving together." Judges and Synchronization Technical Controllers look for the same action to happen at the same time and to create the same picture.

  • Timing: actions should start, finish and change together when the choreography calls for unison.
  • Position: heads, arms, legs and body lines should match.
  • Height: athletes should reach the intended water level together.
  • Spacing & patterns: formations should look clean, balanced and intentional.
  • Important: an intentionally different movement is not a synchronization error if the choreography clearly calls for it.
Small errors distort the picture; obvious errors make movements look one after another; major errors change or omit routine content.

Artistic Impression — What Makes a Routine Look Great?

Artistic Impression is not simply "Did I like the routine?" It evaluates how effectively the athletes communicate the choreography, music and movement to the audience.

Choreography & Musicality

Variety, creativity and innovation; how movements and transitions are woven together; pool coverage; interpretation of the music, its structure, mood, rhythm and accents.

Performance

Total command, confidence, energy, body language, facial expression, focus and the ability to communicate the feeling of the music.

Transitions

The quality and complexity of the movements, propulsions and strokes that connect one Element to the next.

Transitions — The "Glue" of the Routine

Transitions are not empty time between Elements. They are purposeful movement used to connect the routine. Good transitions keep the routine moving, help cover the pool, and make the choreography feel continuous rather than like separate pieces.

  • Use varied and purposeful movements, propulsions and strokes.
  • Finish one section and begin the next smoothly.
  • Avoid visible waiting, awkward stops or unnecessary dead space.
  • Keep formations and spacing clear while changing patterns.
  • The best routines make the next movement feel like a natural continuation of the previous one.

Performance & Expression

Athletes should look confident and fully in control from the beginning to the end. The whole body matters: posture, head and torso position, arms, hands, legs, facial expression and eye focus all help communicate the routine.

High-level performance should look powerful but controlled, energetic but fluid, and expressive without looking forced. Athletes should respond to changes in the music and communicate its mood honestly.

Coach Card & Declared Difficulty

The Coach Card records the declared difficulty and order of the routine's Elements. Technical Controllers verify what is actually performed against what was declared. The practical rule for athletes is simple: Do what you declare, in the declared order.

Hybrid Base Mark

Every Free Hybrid starts from a 0.50 Base Mark. Difficulty is then added through the declared movement-family values and applicable bonuses. If the declared difficulty is not successfully achieved, the Hybrid receives the Base Mark value.

Base Mark — Athlete & Coach Guide

Understanding Base Marks

A Base Mark is not a judgment of an athlete's talent. It is a technical result that tells the team: the declared difficulty was not achieved as declared.

Where does it come from?

The Coach Card declares the difficulty and the order in which the Hybrid movements are performed. Technical Controllers compare the performance in the water with that declaration.

What can trigger it?

  • A declared movement is omitted.
  • The movement or technique performed is different from what was declared.
  • The declared rotation is not completed.
  • A required height, position or other declared criterion is not achieved.
  • The Hybrid is performed in a different order from the Coach Card.

The key rule: Do What You Declare.

World Aquatics states that athletes should perform the skills exactly as declared on the Coach Card and in the declared order. For a Free Hybrid, if the declared difficulty is not successfully achieved, the Hybrid goes to the 0.50 Base Mark.

For Athletes: Learn From the BM

A Base Mark gives you useful information. Look at the video, identify exactly what changed, and train that detail: the height, the rotation, the position, the timing, the connection or the order. The goal is not simply to fear a BM — it is to become precise enough that the declared difficulty becomes reliable under pressure.

For Coaches: Use the Information

A BM can help identify whether the declared difficulty matches the athlete's current level of control. Coaches can use video review and competition feedback to clean the skill, adjust the declaration when appropriate, and build a routine that is both ambitious and dependable.

The Tornadoes “No Base Mark” Mindset

We do not train to be afraid of Base Marks. We train to understand them. Every BM is a chance to ask: What exactly happened? Why did it happen? What do we need to change? Then we go back to the pool, refine the skill and make the performance more precise. Train → Refine → Perform → Learn → Improve.

Junior & Senior — Technical Routines

Junior and Senior also compete in Technical Routines. The Free Hybrid component is:

  • Technical Solo: 1 Free Hybrid + 5 TRE
  • Technical Duet: 1 Free Hybrid + 5 TRE + 1 Pair Acrobatic
  • Technical Mixed Duet: 1 Free Hybrid + 1 Required Hybrid + 3 TRE + 2 Pair Acrobatics
  • Technical Team: 2 Free Hybrids + 5 TRE + 1 Team Acrobatic

Acrobatics are separate from Free Hybrids and are declared separately on the Coach Card. For example, a Junior/Senior Free Team has 6 Free Hybrids + 3 Team Acrobatics.

Free Combination: 12 & Under uses 8 required elements; Youth uses 9 required elements. Junior/Senior do not use Free Combination in the information summarized here.

🌱 FOUNDATION CATEGORY

🌱 Supernovice — The Foundation Year

Supernovice is the initial and most basic competitive level for athletes being introduced to Artistic Swimming. It allows beginners to experience competition without the technical pressure of higher levels.

Purpose

Introduce athletes to competition while building the fundamental skills required for progression.

Age Subdivisions

May include 8&Under, 12&Under and 13&Over, according to the applicable competition structure.

Technical Requirements

Short, simple routines and basic figures, with emphasis on positions, coordination, water confidence and fundamental movement.

Progression

Athletes demonstrating skills beyond the intended Supernovice level may need to progress to Novice or Intermediate.

Foundation First. Progression Follows.

The first year is a crucial developmental year. Athletes develop alignment, body positions, pointed feet, sculling, coordination, water confidence, flexibility, strength and fundamental movement patterns that support more advanced figures, routines and competitive skills.

🔑 Key Junior Technical Routine Rules
1. ORDER IS MANDATORY

For Solo, Duet and Team Technical Routines, the required technical elements must be performed in the exact numerical order shown on the Coach Card.

2. TIME TOLERANCE

The total routine duration has a strict tolerance of ±5 seconds.

3. BASE MARK

If a required element is omitted or its required order is altered, that element receives a Base Mark rather than its declared difficulty.

Coach Card reminder: The Coach Card is not just paperwork. It establishes the declared order of the required elements, so athletes and coaches must rehearse the routine exactly as declared.

Tornadoes Athlete Development

Artistic Swimming Categories & Pathway Guide

From foundational development through Age Group, Youth, Junior, Senior and international performance pathways.

Progression
FOUNDATION → DEVELOPMENT → NATIONAL → ELITE

How the Tornadoes Pathway Works

Athletes develop progressively through technical categories and age-group pathways. Development begins before the formal Age Group pathway: Supernovice, Novice and Intermediate training establish technical skills, artistry, physical literacy, confidence and competition experience before athletes progress into 12&Under, Youth, Junior and Senior pathways.

1. FOUNDATION

Supernovices — establish alignment, body positions, pointed feet, sculling, water confidence, flexibility, strength and fundamental movement patterns.

2. DEVELOPMENT

Novice & Intermediate — build stronger technical control, figures, transitions, propulsion, flexibility and competition readiness.

3. AGE GROUP

12&Under, Youth, Junior and Senior — progressively higher technical, routine and competitive standards.

4. INTERNATIONAL

Development Squad → National Team → Elite National Team, subject to official ASAJ eligibility and qualification standards.

1

Supernovices — The Foundation Year

Supernovice is the initial competitive/development level for athletes being introduced to artistic swimming. The focus is on learning the sport correctly rather than rushing difficulty.

Purpose

Give beginners an appropriate first competition experience while building reliable fundamentals.

Development focus

Alignment, posture, body positions, pointed feet, basic sculling, coordination, water confidence, flexibility and strength.

Foundation First. Progression Follows.

A strong first year creates the foundation for everything that comes after.

2

Novices — Building the Technical Base

Novice athletes move beyond the foundation stage and develop greater control of positions, figures, sculling, propulsion, boosts, transitions and basic routine execution.

  • Greater consistency and body-line control.
  • More demanding figures and transitions.
  • Introduction of stronger routine-memory and competition-style execution.
  • Continued landwork, flexibility and strength development.
3

Intermediate — Technical Progression

Intermediate athletes work toward stronger stability, support sculling, knee extension, clean rotations, vertical control and more complex figure and routine transitions. The goal is reliable execution that supports progression into Age Group competition.

4

Age Group Pathway — 12&Under, Youth, Junior & Senior

Once athletes enter the Age Group pathway, expectations increase across figures, technical elements, routines, difficulty, execution quality, artistic impression and competitive consistency.

12&Under

Build competition-ready figure quality, sculling strength, height, flexibility and clean lines.

Youth (13–15)

Increase technical difficulty, height, flexibility, hybrid understanding and competitive presentation.

Junior

Advanced elements, precision, speed, routine execution and National Team preparation become central.

Senior

Highest domestic age-group demands with emphasis on international-level execution and difficulty.

5

ASAJ International Qualification Pathway

The 2026–2027 ASAJ framework establishes three performance tiers: Development Squad, National Team, and Elite National Team. Qualification depends on the required average score in Figures/Technical Elements and Routine, together with the required minimum executed Degree of Difficulty (DD).

DEVELOPMENT SQUAD

Entry-level international pathway designed to gain regional competition experience and build toward higher standards.

NATIONAL TEAM

Selection to represent Jamaica at official competitions such as CARIFTA, CCCAN and PanAm Aquatics, subject to eligibility.

ELITE NATIONAL TEAM

Highest performance tier, focused on international podium-level preparation, World Cups and World Championships.
